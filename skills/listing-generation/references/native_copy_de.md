# Native German Listing Copy

Use this reference for every DE draft. Preserve locked keywords and improve
the sentence around them; do not substitute a keyword merely because another
term is easier to write.

The model performs this language review; a pipeline PASS verifies mechanical
checks, not idiomatic German. Keep factual and compliance gates authoritative.

## Drafting standard

- Write idiomatic German for a buyer, not a translation of source syntax.
- Give each Bullet one clear question and one coherent answer. Keep all clauses
  in the same semantic lane: compatibility, function, material, package, or care.
- Use one main product noun throughout. Introduce another noun only when it is
  a locked search term or names a genuinely different component.
- Use concise lead phrases that the following clause actually answers. Remove
  duplicated instructions such as `prüfen` followed by `abgleichen`.
- Avoid label-plus-restatement constructions. For example, shorten
  `Passform prüfen: Passend für Auron P58 mit 58-mm-Brühgruppe` to
  `Passend für Auron P58 mit 58-mm-Brühgruppe`; the compatibility phrase itself
  answers the buyer's fit question.
- Prefer concrete verbs over abstract nominalizations. State what the buyer can
  see, do, check, remove, or clean.
- Write Item Highlights as concise feature phrases. Use commas for related
  attributes and a period between independent functions, scenes, or purchase
  reasons when a comma would blur the meaning. Avoid a long explanatory
  sentence. Capitalize the field opening and follow standard German noun
  capitalization.
- Embed a selected keyword in a truthful function or use-case phrase when that
  is its planned placement; do not append a bare keyword or stack aliases.
- Use one winning visible term for each search concept in Item Highlights; do
  not stack aliases or regional variants merely to increase coverage.
- Break up long supplier compounds when ordinary German permits it. Preserve a
  locked compound, but place it in a grammatical clause instead of a keyword list.
- Apply correct capitalization, articles, case, adjective endings, compounds,
  and hyphenation. Do not copy English word order into German.
- Join measurement compounds with standard German hyphenation where required:
  write `58-mm-Siebträger` and `58-mm-Brühgruppe`, not `58mm Siebträger` or
  `58 mm Brühgruppe`. If the supplied keyword uses a different surface form,
  keep it as the recorded keyword and declare the natural German form as its
  variant when that form is allowed; do not claim an exact-form requirement was
  met by a different spelling.
- Do not rotate among `Siebträger`, `Filterhalter`, and another product noun for
  variety. Use an alternate term only when its keyword plan explicitly requires it.
- Treat malformed, truncated, or semantically unclear supplier terms as pending
  facts unless the user explicitly locks the exact term and its meaning is safe.

## Human-read pass

Read every field as a complete unit. Rewrite it when any answer is yes:

1. Would a German seller need to explain what this phrase means?
2. Does a clause exist only to repeat a keyword or model already stated nearby?
3. Does the lead repeat the closing instruction?
4. Does the Bullet switch to a different buyer question after the semicolon?
5. Does the copy sound like catalog attributes joined by commas rather than a
   deliberate product statement?
6. Does Item Highlights use valuable supported facts, or was wording added only
   to approach the character limit?
7. Does the clause merely explain the component's name? For example,
   `Der Doppelauslauf verteilt Espresso auf zwei Ausläufe` can become
   `Espresso fließt aus beiden Ausläufen`; do not infer simultaneous cups,
   even distribution, or better extraction from the outlet count alone.

When a locked keyword sounds awkward, retain the term and first try a new
verb, prepositional phrase, clause order, or related Bullet. Stop adding further
uses when every remaining placement would be unrelated or repetitive.
