# Native US Listing Copy

Use this reference for every US draft. Preserve locked keywords and improve
the sentence around them; do not substitute a keyword merely because another
term is easier to write.

The model performs this language review; a pipeline PASS verifies mechanical
checks, not idiomatic US English. Keep factual and compliance gates authoritative.

## Drafting standard

- Write direct, idiomatic US product copy, not translated supplier English.
- Give each Bullet one buyer question and one coherent answer. Keep supporting
  clauses in the same lane: fit, function, material, package, or care.
- Use one main product noun consistently. Use another only when it is a locked
  search term or the correct name for a different component.
- Prefer active, concrete verbs and ordinary buyer language. Avoid vague phrases
  such as `enhances the experience`, `allows you to`, and `perfect for` when a
  specific observable action is available.
- Prefer a short natural lead over an all-caps instruction label. Do not make a
  lead repeat the claim that follows it: `CHECK CAPACITY: Holds 64 oz` can be
  reduced to `Capacity: 64 oz`, and `POUR CONTROL: Side handle supports pouring`
  to `Use the side handle to pour`. Keep a colon only when the lead adds
  useful buyer context.
- Write Item Highlights as concise feature phrases. Use commas for related
  attributes and a period between independent functions, scenes, or purchase
  reasons when a comma would blur the meaning. Avoid a long explanatory
  sentence. Capitalize the first alphabetic word; do not use Title Case for the
  complete field.
- Keep each phrase readable and grammatically sufficient. Embed a selected
  keyword in a truthful function or use-case phrase when that is its planned
  placement; do not append a bare keyword or stack aliases.
- Use one winning visible term for each search concept in Item Highlights. Do
  not stack aliases such as `Christmas tree`, `Xmas tree`, and `holiday tree`.
- Avoid supplier noun stacks. Preserve a locked multiword keyword, but place it
  inside a grammatical clause rather than extending the noun chain.
- When supported noun fragments describe one product, make their relation
  explicit without moving modifiers: `Pink chenille stems, flower-making kit`
  can become `Flower-making kit with pink chenille stems` when the stems belong
  to that kit. Pink still describes the stems, not every component of the kit.
- Do not rotate among `portafilter`, `filter holder`, and another product noun
  merely for variety. Use an alternate only when its keyword plan requires it.
- Treat malformed, query-order, or semantically unclear terms as pending unless
  the user explicitly locks the exact term and its meaning is safe. When exact
  order is not required, preserve the validated words in natural US syntax.
- Vary sentence structure only where it improves flow; do not repeat template
  leads such as `Designed for`, `Allows you to`, or `Perfect for` across Bullets.

## Human-read pass

Read every field as a complete unit. Rewrite it when any answer indicates a problem:

1. Would a US seller naturally say the sentence aloud? If no, rewrite it.
2. Does a clause exist only to repeat a keyword or model already stated nearby?
3. Does the lead repeat the same instruction at the end?
4. Does the Bullet switch buyer questions after the semicolon?
5. Does the copy sound like search terms joined together rather than product copy?
6. Does Item Highlights use valuable supported facts, or was wording added only
   to approach the character limit?

When a locked keyword sounds awkward, retain the term and first try a different
verb, clause order, prepositional phrase, or related Bullet. For Item
Highlights, place it inside a short function or use-case phrase when possible.
Stop adding further uses when every remaining placement would be unrelated or
repetitive.
